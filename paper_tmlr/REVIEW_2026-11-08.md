Written by a fresh model instance (Claude Fable 5.1) that had not seen the draft before, acting as a
critical TMLR reviewer and as a senior co-author advising on framing. Every number marked [computed]
was recomputed from the raw grid CSVs with the paper's scoring convention (strip `|`, index-aligned,
clip to truth length, k=3 excluded, N<=64 unless stated). The reviewer's scripts are in

Status at review time: the k=1 token-budget rerun was still in progress (effect on pooled numbers is

## PART A: Reviewer critique (most severe first)

**A1. The tier split is confounded with tokenization, and the one control the repo contains says the
confound is large.** Every position-local task uses letters (A–H), every dependency task uses digits.
The tokenization audit (results_clean/_diagnostics/alphabet_tokenization_audit.csv) shows 48 letters
tokenize to 19 tokens without markers and 94 with k=1 markers; digits are 1:1 either way. The paper
admits this in a caption ("Without markers, letters merge into multi-character tokens; markers restore
per-character resolution", Fig. heads32b) but only checks the attention slope with digits, never the
accuracy effect. A digit-alphabet cyclic_shift exists for Qwen2.5-7B (results_clean/cyclic_shift_digits)
and is not in the paper or the report. [computed] Letters vs digits at 7B, Δ by N: N=4 +0.196 vs +0.050;
N=8 +0.231 vs +0.053; N=16 +0.202 vs +0.062; N=32 +0.180 vs +0.069; N=64 +0.142 vs +0.091. Roughly
two-thirds of the headline position-local benefit at 7B disappears when the alphabet already tokenizes
one character per token. The sentence "Markers help position-local tasks ... by +0.09 to +0.20" is
therefore not supported as a statement about position-locality; it is supported as a statement about
letter tasks under BPE. Acceptable wording until the control is run at scale: "On letter-alphabet
position-local tasks, markers raise token accuracy by ...; part of this effect is tokenization (markers
prevent letters from merging into multi-character tokens): with a digit alphabet, the 7B gain falls
from +0.14–0.23 to +0.05–0.09." Fix: run the three position-local tasks with digit alphabets at 32B,
Qwen3.8 and Gemma (3 tasks x 3 models, a few GPU-hours each). This is the single cheapest experiment
with the largest effect on a rejection.

**A2. The reset test is close to tautological as evidence that "severing the dependency restores the
benefit."** [computed] Splitting reset-variant accuracy by position type (N<=32, k in {2,4,8,16},
positions right after a marker = "reset positions", all others = rule-unchanged):
- Qwen2.5-32B running_xor: reset-position accuracy 0.538 (original Bounds) → 0.973 (reset Bounds);
  rule-unchanged positions 0.832 → 0.842. running_sum_local: 0.317 → 0.914 vs 0.616 → 0.573.
  sliding_window_max: 0.675 → 0.885 vs 0.750 → 0.782.
- Qwen3.8: reset positions +0.40 to +0.69; rule-unchanged positions +0.010, −0.078, −0.008.
  Gemma: +0.15 to +0.65 vs +0.013 to +0.029.

About 90% of the Δ increase comes from the positions whose rule was redefined to "copy this digit"
(chance → ~0.97); positions whose rule did not change move by at most ±0.03 (−0.08 at Qwen3.8). At k=1
the reset variant is literally the string-copy task. So the reset test shows that the harm is localized
to the positions whose lookback crosses a marker; it does not show markers becoming beneficial to the
computation once the dependency is gone. Three further problems: (i) the reset prompt alone shifts Base
accuracy (no markers, identical task): 32B running_sum_local −0.044 (z −3.0), 7B copy_previous +0.058
(z 3.4), 7B running_xor +0.054 (z 3.6), Gemma copy_previous −0.078 (z −3.6), as large as the original
dependency effects (+0.009 to +0.046 at 32B) being called "cancelled", so a third of the 32B
"restoration" is Base moving down, not Bounds moving up (Bounds-vs-Bounds: +0.079, +0.057, +0.083);
(ii) reset-instruction compliance at distinguishing positions is poor at k=1 for 32B (follows reset
rule 25%/63%/34%/42% for copy/xor/sum/sliding; follows carry-over 70% on copy_previous) and fine at
k>=4 (0.88–1.00), so k=1 reset cells are scored against a rule the model did not apply; (iii) the
"14 of 15 move toward benefit" count is true but copy_previous_reset remains significantly negative at
3 of 4 models, and at Qwen3.8 compliance with the switch is 97% while rule-unchanged positions still
fall (0.749 → 0.700), so the "rule switch" explanation for copy_previous does not hold there.
Acceptable wording: "Redefining the position after each marker so that it needs nothing from before the
marker removes the harm at exactly those positions (accuracy there rises from near chance to 0.89–0.99
at the three largest models) and leaves accuracy at all other positions unchanged (within ±0.03). The
harm from markers on dependency tasks is therefore confined to the positions whose lookback crosses a
marker." Drop "restores the benefit", the 56–64% / 59–92% arithmetic, and Fig. 3 as a Δ-vs-Δ bar
chart; show per-position accuracy instead. Report the Base shift under the reset prompt and the
compliance table (scripts/audit_reset_instruction_compliance.py exists; extend it beyond 7B).

**A3. "Cancellation" at 32B is an average of significant harm at short N and significant gain at
long N.** [computed] Qwen2.5-32B Δ by N: running_xor −0.113 (N=4, z −5.2), +0.018, +0.033, +0.082,
+0.116 (N=64, z +7.6); running_sum_local −0.050 (z −3.2) → +0.129 (z +10.7); copy_previous −0.017 →
+0.003. Gemma running_sum_local −0.059/−0.102/−0.087 (N=4–16) → +0.042/+0.184 (N=64, z 12.1). Qwen3.8
running_xor +0.003 → +0.187. And the N=64 cells still contain the budget-capped k=1 rows, so these
gains will rise. "The same markers produce no net gain at Qwen2.5-32B" and "becomes harm at smaller
Qwen models and on Gemini" are pooled statements that do not describe any single length. What is
constant: the gap between tier averages at 32B is 0.19, 0.19, 0.22, 0.20, 0.19 at N=4–64 [computed];
at Qwen3.8 excluding copy_previous 0.15–0.22; at Gemma 0.12–0.26. Acceptable wording: "Dependency
tasks gain 0.15–0.22 less than position-local tasks at every length and at every large model; whether
the residual is a loss or a gain depends on length and model (harm at N<=8, gains of +0.12 to +0.19 at
N=64 for 32B, Qwen3.8 and Gemma)." Note the raw-TAR gap is also not scale-comparable (A6).

**A4. The mechanism section is stated above its evidence, and the title generalizes it to models where
it cannot apply.**
- Panel (a) "attention mass on markers rises with density": Cell_Boundary_Attn
  (scripts/run_attention_templated.py:187) is the attention summed over all marker tokens. Going from
  k=16 to k=1 multiplies the marker count by 16 and the mass by ~3, so per-marker attention falls ~5x.
  Nothing here shows markers receive more attention than a content token, which is what "sink" means in
  Xiao et al./Gu et al. Report per-token attention on markers vs on content tokens, or drop "sink" for
  "anchor".
- "Blocking those heads from the markers removes the benefit" (paragraph head): at the primary model it
  removes 0%, 0%, 11%, 32%, 28% while the benefit at N<=16 is +0.26. Say "removes up to a third of the
  benefit at 32B and up to three quarters at 7B". The random-head and non-marker-position controls
  exist only at 7B; a reviewer will ask for them at 32B (cheap).
- "Positional, not induction heads": heads selected for attending to adjacent markers will score high
  on a previous-token test by construction; the 95th-percentile result is partly the selection
  criterion. Call them "local/previous-token heads" and acknowledge this.
- Gu et al. is used correctly (sinks tied to softmax normalization; shown in models <=1B; absent
  without it), and the Section 6 "Scope" paragraph is well hedged. The title ("Structural Tokens as
  Attention Sinks") and the abstract's unqualified "Mechanistically, markers act as attention sinks"
  are not; the model with the largest gain (Qwen3.8, +0.22) has softmax in 16 of 64 layers. Either
  scope the title to Qwen2.5 or remove the mechanism from it. Proposed abstract wording: "In Qwen2.5,
  markers draw attention from local heads ..."
- Also note Gemma-4 has 50 of 60 layers sliding-window; whether sinks form in local windows is itself
  a question.

**A5. "Markers help position-local tasks at every model" overreaches on Phi, Mistral and the
naturalistic task.** Phi: one significant cell of three (+0.048 z 3.1; +0.017 z 1.1; +0.018 z 1.3).
Mistral: significant but decaying to zero by N=64–256. Vowel tagging: 7B −0.083 (z 2.3), 32B +0.012;
the text handles this honestly in Section 4 and Limitations, but intro claim 1 and the abstract do not
carry the scope. Also 7B vowel-tagging Base is 0.438 on a binary task: by the paper's own Kadane
standard (at-chance base is not interpreted) this cell is uninterpretable, so do not quote it as harm
either; say the 32B null is the informative one. Acceptable wording for claim 1: "On in-context
position-local rules with letter alphabets, markers help every Qwen model, Gemma and Gemini by +0.09 to
+0.22, and Mistral and Phi by +0.03 to +0.04 with the gain fading by N=64 at Mistral; a
pretrained-knowledge task (vowel tagging) shows no benefit."

**A6. Raw TAR deltas are not comparable across tiers (chance, floor, ceiling).** Dependency tasks are
binary (chance 0.5) with Base 0.75–0.99 at 14B+/Gemini; position-local Base is 0.3–0.6 with chance
0.125–0.5. Gemini's dependency Bases are 0.90–0.99, so "harm on Gemini" is also "nowhere to go up"; 7B
running_xor Base is 0.41 (below chance), Mistral/Phi dependency Bases 0.32–0.58: floor. A reviewer will
ask for relative error change or a chance-corrected gain alongside Δ, and for the same at-chance
exclusion rule applied to the paper's own 7B/Mistral/Phi dependency cells that is applied to CLRS Kadane.

**A7. Statistics and selection.** (i) Figure 1 stars at |z|>=2 while the text defines Bonferroni at
3.3; at 3.3 the counts are 12 of 18 and 8 negative, not 14 and 10. (ii) k=3 exclusion is post hoc
("powers of two"); including it flips Mistral running_xor (+0.034 z 3.1 → +0.016 z 1.4) and
running_sum_local (−0.023 z −2.7 → −0.016 z −1.9) and Q7 running_xor_reset (1.5 → 2.8) [computed; data
in results_clean/_k3_sensitivity]. Put the k=3-included table in the appendix. (iii) Gemini
copy_previous/running_xor rest on 15 Base trials each. (iv) The k=1 budget cap: every local model's k=1
output at N=64 is truncated (TAR ceiling ~0.906) for all tasks including the position-local ones
[computed]; state the bug and the fix in Setup, not only in the plan.

**A8. "Sparse markers are faster than no markers" is unexplained and will read as a red flag.**
Throughput is constant, so k=8 at N=128 producing output in 0.44x the time means Base generates ~2.3x
more tokens than k=8 for a shorter answer: overlong tails or different tokenization of the output.
Report tokens generated per condition and explain; and note the k=1 ratio at N>=64 (1.3x) is
understated by the cap.

**A9. copy_previous is a different phenomenon and the paper half-says so.** [computed] At 32B, harm is
at k=2 (−0.136) and k=4 (−0.044); k=1, 8, 16 are positive (+0.041, +0.033, +0.020), consistent with
Fig. 2d but inconsistent with the pooled "hurt at every model" reading. Unlike running_xor/sum (where
only cut positions are hurt), copy_previous is hurt at rule-unchanged positions too (7B −0.085, 14B
−0.113, Qwen3.8 −0.225, Gemma −0.078): an off-by-one at the cut propagates through the segment because
the output is a shifted copy of the input. State this as a cursor-slip/desynchronization effect
distinct from the cut effect. Also the 50% "otherwise" rate in the error-recovery analysis is chance
for A=2; say so.

**A10. Smaller items.** "The two current-generation models replicate the result in every pair": Gemma's
reset gains are +0.06 to +0.08 against position-local +0.18, i.e., a third, not a replication of the
32B "56–64%". Appendix per-model figures still show first-batch curves (caption admits it). Intro item
5 (density falls with N) is shown for two tasks at one model and is descriptive by the paper's own CV
table; demote it from the numbered findings. "+0.15 to +0.20 for the larger models" in the abstract
should be +0.15 to +0.22.

   Without it A1 is a likely reject-or-major-revision on its own.
2. Per-position decomposition (free; see Part B) and segment-aligned scoring as a robustness check.

6. Locate marker-attending heads in Qwen3.8's 16 softmax layers and ablate, or add one pure

## PART B: The best story

The pooled heatmap is the wrong unit. Every anomaly listed above (sign flips with N and with model,
Qwen3.8's residual gains, copy_previous, the reset result) resolves when accuracy is read per output
position. [computed, N<=32, k in {2,4,8,16}; "cut" = the position right after a marker, "uncut" = all
others]: on letter position-local tasks the cut position is the best position in the sequence at every
model (random_permutation Base/uncut/cut: 7B 0.35/0.38/0.70; 14B 0.39/0.47/0.83; 32B 0.44/0.55/0.93;
Qwen3.8 0.46/0.61/0.95; Gemma 0.55/0.69/0.97; Gemini 0.54/0.71/0.98; Mistral cyclic_shift
0.32/0.30/0.54; Phi 0.28/0.29/0.45). On digit dependency tasks the same position is at chance at k>=2
at every model (32B running_xor cut 0.47–0.56 vs Base 0.745; running_sum_local 0.29–0.32 vs 0.535;
Gemini running_sum_local 0.48 vs 0.96), while uncut positions gain in strong models (+0.05 to +0.14 at
32B/Qwen3.8/Gemma, +0.15 to +0.20 at N=64 for 32B) and not at all in 7B/Mistral/Phi. Within-segment
gain decays with distance from the marker (k16_uncut ~ Base everywhere). The pooled Δ is just the
1/k-weighted mix of these two numbers, which is why dense k hurts dependency tasks, sparse k helps them,
the tier gap is constant, and Qwen3.8 (large uncut gain) nets positive. Caveat: at 7B the cut-position
jump mostly vanishes with digits (cyclic_shift_digits Base/uncut/cut 0.36/0.41/0.44 vs letters
0.43/0.51/0.77), so for letter tasks the anchor is partly "the marker gives the next letter its own
token". The chance-level result at cut positions is tokenization-independent (digits).

**Candidate 1: the current draft ("help until they cut a dependency; cancellation").** Pitch: markers
help position-local tasks, cutting a dependency cancels the gain, severing it restores it, sinks
explain both. Verdict: the two load-bearing sentences (cancellation, restoration) are each contradicted
by a free re-analysis of the authors' own data (A2, A3), and the tier contrast carries the tokenization
confound (A1). Do not lead with it.

**Candidate 2: "the gap between tiers is constant."** Pitch: markers add a roughly fixed +0.2 to
position-local tasks at every length and large model; a cut dependency subtracts a roughly fixed 0.2,
so the sign of the net effect is incidental. Verdict: the constancy at 32B (0.19–0.22 at every N) is
real and striking, but it is a descriptive regularity in a metric that is not scale-comparable across
tiers (A6), it inherits A1 (the +0.2 is letter-task inflated), and it offers no mechanism. Good as a
supporting figure, not a headline.

**Candidate 3: "markers help position-local computation; dependency tasks are the control."** Pitch: a
cheap single-call intervention with a clean boundary condition. Verdict: safest, but false in detail:
dependency tasks are not a null control (their uncut positions gain as much as position-local uncut
positions at 32B, and their cut positions are destroyed), and it still rests on letter tasks. It also
throws away the paper's most interesting data.

**Candidate 4: mechanism-first (attention sinks).** Weakest: one model family, a total-mass measure
that rises trivially with marker count, ablation explaining 0–32% at the primary model, and the
largest-gain model mostly without softmax.

**Candidate 5 (recommended): position-resolved: "A boundary marker anchors the token after it, unless
that token needs what came before it."** Abstract-level pitch: Inserting a content-free `|` every k
characters into a character-level transformation changes accuracy position by position, not task by
task. The character right after a marker becomes the most accurately transformed position in the
sequence (from 0.44 to 0.93 at Qwen2.5-32B, and at every one of eight models including the weakest)
when its output depends only on itself, and falls to chance at every model when its output depends on
the character before the marker. Positions farther from a marker gain less, and only in stronger
models. Every pooled effect in the paper (gains on substitution tasks, losses on one-step-dependency
tasks at dense spacing, gains on the same tasks at sparse spacing, the constant ~0.2 gap between task
tiers, copy_previous's propagating failure, and the "reset" result) follows from mixing these two
per-position effects in proportion 1/k. Part of the post-marker gain on letter alphabets is
tokenization (the marker gives the following letter its own token; with digits the 7B gain shrinks by
two-thirds), and we separate that share with digit-alphabet controls. In Qwen2.5, local heads attend
from each position to the adjacent marker, and masking that attention removes up to a third (32B) or
three quarters (7B) of the gain. Practical rule: place markers so that no position's dependency crosses
one; the density that achieves this is the sparsest that still anchors enough positions.

Why this one: it is the only framing that is true at every model (Mistral and Phi, where the *entire*
benefit sits at the post-marker position; Qwen3.8, where the residual dependency gain is the
within-segment term; Gemini, where cut positions go to chance even at ceiling), it turns the reset
experiment from a near-tautology into a confirmation of a per-position prediction, it absorbs the
tokenization confound as a measured component instead of a hidden one, and TMLR's criterion (claims
supported by evidence) is met by data already on disk.

Title options: "The Token After the Bar: Where Content-Free Boundary Markers Help and Hurt
Character-Level Transformations" or "Boundary Markers Anchor the Next Position and Blind It to the
Last." Figure 1: two rows (letter position-local tasks; digit dependency tasks) by eight model columns,
each cell three marks (Base, Bounds at within-segment positions, Bounds at the position after a marker)
pooled N<=32, k in {2,4,8,16}, with the digit-alphabet position-local tasks added as a third row once
run; a small side panel showing per-k pooled Δ against the 1/k mixture prediction. Figure 2: the reset
variant per position (reset positions chance → 0.97; other positions flat). Figure 3: mechanism,
scoped to Qwen2.5, with per-token marker attention. Figure 4: density-by-length and cost.

Claims to drop or weaken for this to hold at every model: drop "cancels"/"restores the benefit" and
the 56–64% / 59–92% arithmetic; drop "attention sinks" from title and abstract (keep "local heads
attend to adjacent markers" for Qwen2.5 only); restate claim 1 as letter-alphabet in-context rules
pending digit controls, with the tokenization share quantified; restate copy_previous as a
desynchronization effect; replace "14 of 15 move toward benefit" with the per-position statement;
report Bonferroni-consistent stars, k=3-included numbers, relative-error metrics, and the Base shift
under the reset prompt. Before adopting, reproduce the per-position numbers inside the paper's
pipeline (the reviewer's scripts had to strip spaces from Qwen2.5 cyclic_shift ground truth; scoring
is index-aligned after stripping, so a segment-aligned variant should be checked) and run the
digit-alphabet control at 32B: if the post-marker anchor survives with digits at 32B the story is as
pitched; if it shrinks as at 7B, the honest headline becomes "a boundary marker is also a tokenization
intervention", which is still a publishable and more useful result than the current draft.
